How to Merge Lecture Notes into One PDF

PBy the PDFNest Team·September 11, 2026·5 min read

Lecture slides, tutorial sheets and photographed notes are easier to review when they are organised into one useful study file. Here is a practical way to merge lecture notes into one PDF, with checks that prevent missing pages, duplicate handouts and the wrong week order.

Choose a useful scope for your study file

Decide what the combined PDF is for before adding files. A single week’s revision pack, a topic summary and a whole semester archive need different structures. If you mainly read on a phone, a smaller file for each topic can be easier to navigate than one enormous document containing every course resource.

Create a working folder and copy in the notes you want to include. Keep the originals elsewhere. Use consistent names such as 01-week-01-slides.pdf, 02-week-01-tutorial.pdf and 03-week-02-slides.pdf. Leading zeroes help avoid confusing sequences when filenames are sorted, but you should still check the order in the merge tool.

Remove superseded downloads from the working set. Two files with nearly identical names may be different revisions of the same lecture. Compare their opening pages and any correction notes before choosing which version belongs in your study packet.

Convert photographs and other files first

The Merge PDF tool takes PDF files. If some handwritten notes are JPG or PNG photos, make a PDF of those images with JPG to PDF first. Put each photo in order, check its orientation and download the result. You can then combine that file with your existing lecture PDFs.

For notes written in a document editor, use its PDF export option where available. For presentation slides, choose the PDF or handout export that gives you a readable layout. A six-slide handout may save pages but make equations or small diagram labels difficult to read on a phone. Check a sample before exporting the whole set.

Do not turn an existing text PDF into screenshots simply to merge it. Keeping its original PDF pages avoids an unnecessary conversion. Photographed handwriting remains an image after conversion, so merging those pages with searchable slides will not automatically make the handwriting searchable.

Merge the PDFs in the order you want to study

  1. Open Merge PDF.
  2. Select your lecture PDFs, tutorial sheets and any photo PDFs you prepared.
  3. Drag the files up or down in the list to set their sequence. Check the actual list rather than assuming the file picker kept your selection order.
  4. Select Merge PDF and wait for the result.
  5. Select Download merged PDF and save it with a descriptive name.

The merge combines every page of the first file, then every page of the next. It does not alternate slides and handwritten notes from separate documents. If you want notes inserted after particular slides, merge the documents first and then arrange individual pages with Organize PDF.

Before processing, write down the source page counts. Three PDFs containing 18, 6 and 12 pages should give you a 36-page result. This simple check catches many accidental omissions without needing to compare every slide immediately.

Tidy the combined document

Open the download and look at the boundaries between files. These are the places where duplicate title slides, blank scanner pages and unexpected ordering are easiest to miss. A repeated cover may be useful as a section divider; a repeated entire lecture usually is not.

Use Remove Pages for unwanted pages, or use the visual organizer when several pages need moving. Refer to the actual PDF page positions, which can differ from the slide numbers printed on the page. For example, the third PDF page may contain a slide labelled “1” after two introductory pages.

If page numbers would help your revision group refer to the same passage, add them after the order is final using Page Numbers. Inspect the placement so it does not cover slide footers, equations or handwritten notes. Renumbering before deleting pages creates an avoidable mismatch.

Preserve search and readable diagrams

Merging copies PDF pages rather than rebuilding every page as a photograph. Existing selectable text generally remains available, but check search and copying in the downloaded result. Bookmarks, form behaviour and document-level navigation should not be assumed to transfer perfectly between unrelated source files.

Try searching for a word from an early lecture and one from a later lecture. Then inspect a detailed graph or equation at normal reading size and at a closer zoom. If one source file was already blurry, merging cannot restore the missing detail. Replace it with a clearer export or scan when possible.

Be careful about compressing a text-based study packet. PDFNest’s Compress PDF rebuilds pages as images at every setting, which removes selectable text. If search matters, keep the merged original and reduce the scope of your packet, or use smaller source exports instead. A smaller download is not always a better revision file.

Handle large files and save a reliable copy

The merge runs on your device, so a long packet with many high-resolution scans can put pressure on browser memory. If processing fails, keep the source files, close unrelated tabs and try smaller groups. A desktop browser may be more practical for a large set than a phone with limited available memory.

On a phone, use the system file picker to choose PDFs from your files app. A document stored only in cloud storage may need to download to your device first. After merging, locate the actual saved PDF and reopen it so you know where your revision copy is stored.

Use a filename that states the course, topic and version, for example Biology-Cell-Division-Revision-v1.pdf. If you share it with classmates, make sure it is material you are permitted to share. Keep the original lecture files so you can replace a corrected handout later without rebuilding your collection from screenshots.

Frequently asked questions

Can I merge slides and handwritten notes?

Yes. Export slides to PDF and convert note photographs to a PDF first, then combine the PDFs in the order you need.

Will merging make handwritten pages searchable?

No. Merging does not perform OCR. Existing selectable text can remain searchable, but images of handwriting stay images.

Can I insert a notes page between two slides?

Yes. Merge the PDFs first, then use Organize PDF to move individual pages into the desired positions.
