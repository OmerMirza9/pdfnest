How to Crop a PDF and Trim Its Margins

PBy the PDFNest Team·November 18, 2026·5 min read

Wide white borders make a PDF hard to read on a phone, slides exported to PDF often arrive framed in empty space, and scans include a strip of scanner lid around the paper. Cropping fixes all of these in seconds without touching the quality of the page. There is one thing to understand before you start, though: cropping hides part of a page, it does not delete it.

What cropping a PDF actually does

Every PDF page has an invisible frame that tells viewers and printers which part of the page to show. Cropping moves that frame inwards. The page is not re-drawn or converted to an image, so text stays sharp and selectable, links keep working and photos keep their full resolution.

That makes cropping very different from cropping a photo. When you crop an image, the pixels outside the selection are thrown away and the file gets smaller. When you crop a PDF, the content outside the frame is still in the file — it is simply not displayed. That is why a cropped PDF is roughly the same size as the original.

It also explains the right use for each tool. Use PDF cropping to change how pages look: trim borders, focus on the content, make pages fit a screen. To make a file smaller, use Compress PDF. To remove content for good, cropping is the wrong tool, as section five explains.

Crop a PDF in your browser

Open Crop PDF and drop your file in. A preview of the first page appears, and four boxes let you set how many millimetres to trim from the top, right, bottom and left. As you type, the preview shades the area that will be cut away, so you can see exactly what you will get before anything is created.

Margins are measured as the page looks on screen. If a page in your document has been rotated, “top” still means the top you see, not the edge the page was originally drawn from. That sounds obvious, but simpler tools get it wrong and trim the wrong side of rotated scans.

When you are happy with the preview, crop and download. The file is processed entirely on your device — useful for contracts, statements and anything else you would rather not upload to a website just to tidy its margins.

Let the tool find the white margins

Guessing millimetres is fiddly, so the tool can measure them for you. Choose “Detect white margins on page 1” and it finds where the content starts and ends on the first page, then fills in the four margins with a little breathing room left around the content.

Check the preview afterwards and adjust any side by hand. Detection looks for anything that is not white, so a page number in the corner, a faint scanner shadow or a coloured header bar counts as content and limits how far that side can be trimmed. Increase the margin on that side if you want the extra removed anyway.

Crop some pages, not all

By default the same margins apply to every page. To crop only some, enter a range in the pages box — for example 1-3, 8. Pages outside the range are left exactly as they were, which is what you want when a document mixes portrait text pages with a landscape chart or a full-bleed cover.

For documents with several different layouts, crop in passes: crop the first group of pages, download, then load the result and crop the next group with different margins. Each pass only changes the pages you name.

If a margin would remove the whole page — a 150 mm trim on a small page, say — that page is skipped rather than broken, and the result tells you how many were skipped. Reduce the margins for those pages and run another pass.

Cropping is not redaction

Because cropping only hides content, anything outside the crop frame can be brought back by anyone with PDF editing software. Never crop a page to hide an account number, an address or a signature before sending it. The information is still in the file.

To share a document without certain content, remove it properly. Take out whole pages with Remove Pages, or go back to the source document, delete the information there and export a new PDF. For a scan, crop the image before it becomes a PDF — image cropping does discard pixels — using Crop Image, then convert it.

The same applies to metadata. A PDF can carry an author name and other details that never appear on the page. If you are tidying a document before sharing it, PDF Metadata shows those properties and can remove them.

Check the cropped file

Open the result and look at a few pages, not just the first. Check that no text is cut at the edges — especially page numbers and footnotes near the bottom — and that pages outside your range were left alone.

Print a test page if the document is going to paper. Printers have their own unprintable edge, so a page cropped very tightly may lose a sliver of content when printed even though it looks fine on screen. Leaving a few millimetres of margin avoids that.

Keep the original. Cropping is reversible only in the sense that the hidden content is still there; if you need a different crop later, starting again from the original is simpler than adjusting a file that has already been cropped. Frequently asked questions

Does cropping a PDF reduce its quality?

No. Cropping changes the visible area of each page without re-rendering it, so text stays selectable and images keep their resolution.

Is the cropped-away part deleted from the PDF?

No. It is hidden, not removed, and editing software can reveal it again. Never use cropping to hide confidential information.

Will cropping make my PDF file smaller?

Not noticeably, because the hidden content stays in the file. To reduce the file size, compress the PDF or remove pages you do not need.
