How to Convert a PDF to Black and White

PBy the PDFNest Team·November 18, 2026·5 min read

Some upload portals and archives insist on black and white documents, colour pages waste expensive ink, and a scan of yellowed paper is easier to read once the colour is gone. Converting a PDF takes a minute — but first check whether you need to convert at all, and then choose between two quite different results: grayscale and true black and white.

Check whether you need to convert at all

If you only want to print without colour, you do not need a new file. Open the print dialog and look for a grayscale, black and white or “colour: off” setting — Windows, macOS and most printer drivers have one. The PDF stays in colour on screen and prints in grey.

Convert the file itself when the black and white version has to leave your hands: an upload portal that rejects colour scans, a records system that requires monochrome documents, an archive that must not depend on colour, or a print shop that charges for colour pages. In those cases the PDF has to be black and white before it is sent.

Converting also helps readability. A scan of an old receipt, a faded carbon copy or a page photographed under yellow light often looks clearer once the tint is removed — especially in high-contrast black and white mode.

Grayscale or black and white?

The two options sound similar and produce very different documents. Grayscale replaces every colour with a matching shade of grey, so photos, charts and shading stay readable. Black and white makes every point either pure black or pure white, removing every shade in between.

DocumentBest modeWhy
Report or slides with photos and chartsGrayscaleKeeps shading so images stay readable
Scanned forms, receipts, lettersBlack and whiteRemoves grey paper and makes text crisp
Faint pencil or carbon copiesBlack and whiteBoosts contrast on light marks
Anything with a signature to verifyGrayscaleKeeps the pen’s natural variation

If you are unsure, try both. Each conversion takes seconds, and comparing two results side by side tells you more than any rule of thumb.

Convert the PDF in your browser

Open PDF to Black and White and drop in your file. Choose Grayscale or Black & white, then pick the quality: Standard works for most documents, while High keeps small print sharper at the cost of a larger file.

Convert and download. Every page is processed on your own device, so the document is never uploaded — worth knowing when the file is a medical record, a statement or an ID scan, which are exactly the documents portals tend to demand in black and white.

The result shows the original and new file sizes before you download, which helps if the destination also has a size limit. If the converted file is still too large, the guide to getting a PDF under 1MB covers the next steps.

What happens to text and file size

To remove colour reliably from everything on a page — text, vector graphics, photos and backgrounds — each page is rebuilt as an image in its new colours. The pages look right in every viewer and on every printer, but the text becomes part of the picture: you can no longer select, copy or search it.

File size depends on what you started with. Scans and photo-heavy PDFs are images already, so converting them usually keeps the size similar or, in black and white mode, makes it smaller. A PDF of typed text is stored very compactly, and turning its pages into images can make it noticeably larger.

For a text document headed to a printer, that is another reason to print in grayscale rather than convert. For a scan headed to a portal, converting is usually the right call, and black and white mode is often the most compact result.

Make the result searchable again

If you need to search or copy text from the converted file, run it through OCR PDF. Optical character recognition reads the words on each page image and adds an invisible text layer, so the document becomes searchable while keeping its new black and white look.

Black and white mode tends to help OCR. Crisp black text on a clean white background is exactly what recognition software reads best, so a faded scan converted to black and white and then OCR’d is often more searchable than the original colour scan was.

The guide to making a scanned PDF searchable explains the OCR step and how to check its accuracy.

Check the converted file

Open the result and zoom into the smallest text, a signature and any chart. In black and white mode, very light marks — a pale stamp, a faint pencil note — can disappear entirely. If something important vanished, convert the original again in grayscale instead.

Check the page count and the order, then check the size against any upload limit. If a portal asks for both black and white and a maximum size, convert first and compress second, rather than the other way round.

Keep the colour original. A black and white copy is fine for submission, but colour carries information — highlighted terms, coloured chart lines, a red stamp — that you may need later.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to convert a PDF to print it in black and white?

No. Most print dialogs have a grayscale or black and white option. Convert the file only when you need to send or upload a black and white copy.

Will the text still be searchable after converting?

No. PDFNest rebuilds each page as an image to remove the colour, so the text becomes part of the picture. Run OCR on the result to make it searchable again.

Will converting to black and white make my PDF smaller?

Often for scans, especially in black and white mode. A PDF of typed text can get larger, because its pages become images. Compare the size shown before downloading.
