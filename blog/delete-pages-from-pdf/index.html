2. Choosing which pages to remove

The format is the same comma-and-dash style used everywhere: 2 deletes page 2; 5-7 deletes pages 5, 6 and 7; combine them freely — 2, 5-7, 10. One thing to watch: page numbers refer to the PDF's physical pages (what your reader shows as 1, 2, 3…), which may differ from printed page numbers on the document itself. Skim the file once to confirm before deleting.

3. Common cases: blanks, duplicates, covers

  • Blank pages from scanning: double-sided scanners often insert an empty page after every sheet. Note the blank page numbers and delete them in one pass.
  • Duplicate scans: scanned the same sheet twice? Delete the copy and keep the better one.
  • Cover sheets and fax headers: usually page 1 — 1 in the box and it's gone.
  • Outdated sections: remove the old pages, then merge in the replacements for a clean final document.

4. Keep-only-a-few? Use Split instead

If you'd be deleting 50 pages to keep 3, flip the job around: the Split tool extracts the pages you want in one step. Delete = remove what you list; Split = keep what you list. Pick whichever needs less typing.
