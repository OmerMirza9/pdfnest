2. Getting the look right (size, opacity, angle)

  • Opacity is the key setting. Around 20–30% reads clearly without making the document hard to read. Go higher only when the warning matters more than readability.
  • 45° diagonal is the classic look — it crosses the text without aligning with any one line.
  • Size: big enough to span most of the page (the default ~50pt suits A4); a watermark that's too small just looks like stray text.
  • Colour: red signals warnings (CONFIDENTIAL), grey is professional and subtle (DRAFT), and a brand colour works for ownership stamps.

3. Common watermark texts and when to use them

  • DRAFT — stops a work-in-progress being mistaken for the final version.
  • CONFIDENTIAL — signals restricted distribution on contracts, finances and HR documents.
  • PAID / COPY / VOID — marks invoices and receipts to prevent reuse.
  • Your name or company — discourages portfolios, reports and proposals being passed off elsewhere.
  • SAMPLE — lets you share previews of paid work safely.

4. What a watermark can and can't protect

Be realistic: a watermark is a strong deterrent, not encryption. It makes misuse obvious and attributable, which stops most casual reuse. But a determined person with editing software can attempt to remove one. For documents that genuinely must not be opened by the wrong people, combine the watermark with password protection — the watermark handles attribution, the password handles access.

The bottom line

Watermarking a PDF takes under a minute: type your text, tune the opacity and angle, download. Keep opacity subtle, go diagonal, and pair it with a password when the stakes are high — all free, all in your browser.
