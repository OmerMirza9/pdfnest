    Turn pictures of text into editable text

    A photo of a receipt, a scanned contract, a screenshot of a paragraph — to your computer these are just images. The words inside them aren't text you can select or search; they're pixels. OCR (Optical Character Recognition) looks at those pixels and works out the letters, turning the picture back into real, editable text.

    PDFNest runs a full OCR engine (Tesseract, compiled to WebAssembly) inside your browser. That's unusual — most OCR sites upload your document to a server. Here, your file is read locally, so even an ID, a bank statement or a confidential contract never leaves your device.

    Getting the most accurate results

    • Use a sharp, high-contrast scan. Clean black-on-white text reads far better than a dim phone photo.
    • Straighten the page. Skewed or rotated text lowers accuracy — crop and align first if you can.
    • Match the language. Selecting the correct language helps the engine recognise accented characters and word shapes.
    • Printed beats handwritten. OCR is built for printed type; handwriting is much less reliable.

    Already have a PDF that contains real (selectable) text? You don't need OCR — the faster PDF to Text tool extracts it directly. Use this OCR tool when the PDF is a scan and copy-paste gives you nothing.

    Image to Text — FAQ

    How do I convert an image to text for free?

    Add a JPG or PNG above, choose the language and click Extract text. OCR runs in your browser — copy the result or download it as a .txt file. Nothing is uploaded.

    Can I extract text from a scanned PDF?

    Yes — the tool OCRs each page of a scanned PDF and turns it into editable text.

    Is the OCR done on a server?

    No. It runs entirely in your browser with WebAssembly, so your files never leave your device.

    Which languages are supported?

    English plus Spanish, French, German, Italian and Portuguese. Pick the one that matches your document.

    Why isn't OCR 100% accurate?

    OCR reads pixels, so quality matters. Sharp, straight, high-contrast scans are most accurate; blurry photos and handwriting are harder.
