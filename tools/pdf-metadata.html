    What your PDF says about you

    Every PDF can carry document properties that never appear on its pages: a title, the author's name, a subject, keywords, the app and version that created it, and the dates it was created and last changed. Many apps also add a second, hidden copy in an XMP metadata block. Recipients can see all of it in their reader's properties panel — including a personal or company name you may not have meant to share.

    Use this tool to check those properties before sending a file, give a document a proper title (it is what many readers show in the window or tab), or remove everything in one click. Only the properties change; the pages stay exactly as they were, and the work happens entirely in your browser.

    What metadata removal doesn't cover

    Names that appear on the pages — in a header, a comment or a signature — are page content, not metadata, and stay put. For a password, use Protect PDF; to send only part of a document, use Split PDF.

    PDF Metadata — FAQ

    What is PDF metadata?

    Information stored about the document rather than on its pages: title, author, subject, keywords, the creating app and dates.

    How do I remove metadata from a PDF?

    Drop it in, click Remove all metadata, and save. The properties and the XMP block are removed in your browser.

    Does removing metadata change the pages?

    No. Only the document properties change.

    Does it remove names on the pages?

    No — names in headers, comments or signatures are page content, not metadata.
