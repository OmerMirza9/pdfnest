      Lock a completed form before you send it

      A fillable PDF keeps its answers in interactive fields. That is convenient while you fill it in, but it means the recipient — or anyone the file is forwarded to — can click a box and change what you wrote, and some viewers and printers display fields differently or not at all. Flattening draws each field's current value onto the page and removes the field, so the form looks the same everywhere and can no longer be edited in a reader.

      Text boxes, checkboxes, radio buttons and drop-down choices are all flattened as they appear. Everything happens in your browser, so personal details in the form are never uploaded.

      What flattening is — and isn't

      Flattening prevents casual edits, not determined ones: someone with PDF editing software can still change the page. To control who can open the document, add a password with Protect PDF after flattening. Flattening also cannot be undone, so keep your fillable copy. Need to fill the form first? Edit PDF adds text to forms that have no fields.

      Flatten PDF — FAQ

      What does flattening a PDF do?

      It turns form fields and your answers into ordinary page content, so they stay visible but can't be clicked or changed.

      Will my answers stay?

      Yes — text, ticks and selected options are drawn onto the page. Save the filled form first, then flatten that copy.

      Does flattening make a PDF secure?

      It stops casual edits, but editing software can still change the page. Use Protect PDF to control who can open it.

      Does it flatten comments?

      No — this tool flattens form fields. Comments, sticky notes and markup are left as they are.
